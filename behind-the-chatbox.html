01 / The human story

It started with a phone call.

As an Electrical Engineering student with a minor in Artificial Intelligence, I use AI frequently. Yet I realized I could not clearly explain the difference between APIs, machine learning, deep learning and LLMs. My dad, an electronics engineer, helped me understand some of these ideas through familiar examples from Colombia. This project is my attempt to share that experience with someone who has never studied AI.

How can we make thoughtful decisions about AI if we don't understand the basics of how it works?

You do not need to know how to code to explore this website.

02 / A little history

AI did not appear overnight.

1950s

Can machines think?

Alan Turing proposed a way to discuss machine intelligence in 1950. The Dartmouth research project in 1956 helped establish AI as a field.

1950s–1990s

Learning from examples

Early neural networks and other machine learning methods developed alongside approaches based on explicit rules.

2010s

Deep learning advances

More computing power, data and improved methods helped neural networks achieve major advances in vision, speech and language.

2017

The Transformer

A new architecture based on attention helped models process relationships between tokens efficiently.

2022–2023

AI becomes conversational

ChatGPT launched in 2022; Claude and Gemini followed in 2023, bringing powerful models to more people through accessible interfaces.

APIs developed alongside software systems; they are not a stage in the evolution of AI.

03 / APIs

How do systems talk to each other?

An application programming interface (API) defines how software can request information or actions from another system. Imagine a delivery company like Rappi sharing selected order statistics with an authorized business. In Colombia, a similar idea can help explain authorized access to vehicle records in systems such as RUNT.

06 / Large language models

One token at a time

Large language models (LLMs) are deep learning models trained on large collections of data to learn patterns in language. Many generate text by predicting a next token, which can be a word or part of a word, and repeating the process. Modern assistants can also use other modalities and tools.

07 / Putting it together

What happens behind the chatbox?

1. Your request

You enter a message in an application.

2. The service

Software sends the request to a model service, often through an API or internal interface.

3. The model

A trained neural network processes the input and generates output, often token by token.

4. Your response

The application displays the result. You decide whether to trust, verify or use it.

ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude are assistant products associated with families of AI models. They may also use search, tools, safety systems and other components. An API is an interface, not a model.

AI → Machine learning → Deep learning → Many modern LLMs

These are nested categories. APIs sit alongside them as a way for software to communicate.

09 / A human question

Using AI is not the same as understanding it.

Throughout our course, we explored how technologies spread and how their creators can lose control over their uses. Mustafa Suleyman calls attention to this containment problem. As AI becomes part of education, work and everyday decisions, understanding its foundations may help us ask better questions about accuracy, privacy, dependence and responsibility. But literacy alone cannot solve every problem: institutions and developers also have responsibilities.

My hope is that this experience makes AI feel less mysterious without making it seem less powerful. We do not all need to become AI engineers, but we should be able to question the tools we rely on.

If AI becomes part of almost everything we do, should understanding it become part of everyone's education?
